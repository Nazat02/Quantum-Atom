## 💔 The problem

Remember the atom from school? A dot in the middle. A few smaller dots circling it like tiny planets.

**It's wrong.** Your teacher knew it. The textbook knew it. It stayed on the page because the truth is hard to draw, and nobody gave you a better picture.

So a whole generation grew up believing:

- electrons *orbit* the nucleus (they don't),
- atoms are *mostly solid stuff* (they're almost entirely empty),
- chemistry is *memorization* (it's geometry you were never shown).

Students can recite "2, 8, 8, 18" but can't say **why water is bent**, why **helium never reacts**, or why **neon signs glow orange**. The tools that *do* show the truth are heavy, licence-gated desktop software built for people who already understand it.

> **There was nothing in between: something you could open in a browser, in ten seconds, and just *get it*.**

---

## ✨ The idea

Open a link. Pick any element. Watch its electrons appear, not as dots on rails, but as glowing clouds of probability shaped by the mathematics of quantum mechanics. Spheres. Dumbbells. Cloverleaves. Rings.

Then keep going:

- Snap two atoms together and watch a **molecule** form.
- Type the name of *anything* and **build it**.
- Dive into the **nucleus**, past the protons and neutrons, down to the **quarks**.
- Flip a switch and see the **antimatter** version of the same thing.

No install. No account. No equations required. Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom.

## 📖 The story

Quantum Atom is a journey in four chapters.

| | Chapter | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The Atom** | *"What does an electron actually look like?"* |
| **2** | **The Molecule** | *"Why do atoms stick together, and why are the shapes what they are?"* |
| **3** | **The Nucleus** | *"What's inside the middle?"* |
| **4** | **The Mirror** | *"What if everything were made of antimatter?"* |

The landing page walks through history: **Democritus** guessing in 400 BCE, **Dalton's** billiard balls, **Thomson's** plum pudding, **Rutherford's** gold-foil shock, **Bohr's** planetary orbits, and finally **Schrödinger, Heisenberg and Born**, who threw out the orbits altogether.

## 🧭 A quick tour: your first five minutes

1. **Pick hydrogen.** One electron, one perfect sphere.
2. **Press → to step through the table.** Helium completes the first shell, lithium starts a new one. That's *why the periodic table has rows*.
3. **Jump to carbon or oxygen.** The dumbbell-shaped **p orbitals** appear.
4. **Try iron.** The cloverleaf-shaped **d orbitals** show up.
5. **Open Molecules, then water.** See the bend, then run the **VSEPR lesson**.
6. **Finish in the Nucleus Explorer.** Keep scrolling until you hit quarks.

## 🔤 The three shapes, in plain words

| Orbital | Shape | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **s** | A sphere | The simplest cloud, the same in every direction. |
| **p** | A dumbbell (three, at right angles) | Where much of chemistry happens: carbon, nitrogen, oxygen bonding. |
| **d** | A cloverleaf (five) | Why transition metals have colour, magnetism and catalytic power. |

## ❓ FAQ

**Is it really free?** Yes. Open the link and use it.

**Do I need to install anything?** No. Any modern browser works.

**Does it work on a phone?** Yes, with touch rotate and pinch-to-zoom.

**Are the shapes real or artistic?** They are drawn from quantum probability distributions, so they are the shapes the theory predicts, not decoration.

**Can I use it in a class?** For learning, yes, by opening the official link. For hosting elsewhere or commercial use, see [License & Credit](#-license--credit).

**Why do orbitals look like fuzzy clouds?** An electron has no exact position. The cloud shows *where it's likely to be*.

## 🚀 Run it in 30 seconds

**Easiest:** open the [live demo](https://nazat02.github.io/Quantum-Atom/).

**Locally:** every tool is a single self-contained HTML file. No build step, no bundler, no install.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/nazat02/Quantum-Atom.git
cd Quantum-Atom
open index_dark.html      # or index_light.html
```

> 💡 *three.js and fonts load from a CDN, and Build mode's lookups need a connection, so the first run should be online.*

## 🧱 Under the hood

- **three.js r128** for 3D rendering
- **Custom GLSL shaders** for point-cloud orbitals (additive blending, per-point sizing) and Fresnel-shaded meshes in Ultra Mode
- **Vanilla JavaScript**, one self-contained HTML file per tool
- **PubChem** REST API for Build mode
- **Zero build step**

```
Quantum-Atom/
├── index.html                  # theme-aware redirect to the landing page
├── index_dark.html             # landing page: story, history, Bohr vs Quantum
├── index_light.html
├── simulator_dark.html         # atoms · molecules · build · lessons · compare · analysis
├── simulator_light.html
├── nucleus_explorer.html       # nucleus → nucleons → quarks
├── nucleus_explorer_light.html
└── images/
```
